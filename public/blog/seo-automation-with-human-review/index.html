How we automated SEO for a finance firm without losing the human review

One dashboard now plans the month, researches, writes and fact-checks. A person still decides what gets published. Here is what we built for a cross-border CFO firm, and what we learned.

AT
Abhuday TripathiFounder, Groviadigi · 15 November 2026
8 min read
Short answer

We built Growth Center, an SEO dashboard for a cross-border CFO firm, because no single tool covered the whole job. It pulls real data from Google Search Console, Analytics and a rank tracker, plans a 30-day strategy, writes each blog with research, fact-checks it against official sources with two AI models, and gives the team 48 hours to approve, reject, pause or reschedule it. The machine does the repetitive work. People keep the final say.

Why SEO by hand stopped working

The client is a cross-border finance firm. Its readers are founders, NRIs and finance professionals, and plenty of them will spot a wrong tax rate in the first paragraph. So every blog needs research, every figure needs a source, and every rule needs to be current. That makes SEO slow.

Before Growth Center, the work lived in spreadsheets: a keyword tool in one tab, Google Search Console in another, and a writer waiting on both. None of it was hard. It was scattered. Most of the week went into moving information from one place to another.

So we asked a simple question: what if one dashboard held the plan, the data, the writing and the review, and only called a person when a real decision was needed?

What Growth Center does

1. It plans the month from real data

Every month the dashboard collects numbers from each connected tool: clicks and impressions from Search Console, traffic from Google Analytics, rankings and AI visibility from the rank tracker, and the questions clients asked in meetings. From that it drafts a 30-day plan covering keywords, a blog calendar, AEO and GEO targets, backlinks and technical fixes.

Two rules keep the plan honest. It may only use numbers from the data it was given, and every target must beat last month’s actual result. When a number is missing, the plan says DATA MISSING instead of guessing.

2. One goal, three pillars: SEO, AEO and GEO

We gave the whole system one goal: grow client conversions through blogs, SEO, AEO and GEO. Every keyword and every blog in the plan names the pillar it serves.

If you are new to the terms: SEO gets a page ranked on Google, AEO (answer engine optimisation) gets it picked as the direct answer in snippets and voice results, and GEO (generative engine optimisation) gets the firm named when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity. Buyers now move between all three, so the dashboard reports on each one separately.

3. It writes and fact-checks each blog

About 72 hours before a blog’s slot, the dashboard researches the topic: the pages ranking now in the US and India, Google’s “People also ask” questions, and primary sources such as the IRS, CBDT and RBI. Then it writes the draft in the firm’s house voice, with a direct answer at the top, an FAQ and one clear call to action.

Before anyone on the team sees it, the draft is checked against official sources. Two different AI models check it in turn, and a blog that cannot be fully verified is never published. A plagiarism check runs as well.

This matters more in finance than almost anywhere else. A confident paragraph with an outdated deadline does real damage to trust, and no amount of traffic makes up for that.

4. People review, and the dashboard waits for them

This is the part we refused to automate away. Each blog opens a 48-hour review. The team can approve it, reject it with feedback (the dashboard rewrites it and shows what changed), pause it, or move it to another day. When someone reschedules, the dashboard suggests free days that still leave time for writing and review.

Tax, legal and compliance blogs never publish on their own. They wait for a named CA or CPA reviewer.

5. It learns from the team’s edits

Every edit a reviewer makes is recorded: how much of the text changed, which sentences, whether a number or a heading moved. Once a day those edits become lessons for the writer, so the next draft needs less fixing. If someone uploads a blog they wrote themselves, the dashboard can optimise it for search and AI answers while keeping their sentences and facts.

6. It does the follow-up nobody enjoys

After a blog goes live, the dashboard submits the page through IndexNow, resubmits the sitemap, adds the keyword to rank tracking and links the new post from older related posts. Every morning it also scans the last 24 hours of tax and compliance news, so the team knows what is worth writing about today.

What we learned building it

Automate the steps, not the judgement. The dashboard is fast at research, drafting and checking. It is the wrong place to decide whether a sensitive tax post is ready for clients. That stays with people.

Make every number traceable. Each metric in the plan carries its source and date. A plan that guesses is worse than a plan that admits a gap.

Plan for pauses. AI work costs money. A paused or restarted job picks up from its saved steps, so nothing is paid for twice.

Respect what Google actually rewards. Google’s own guidance says it looks for helpful, reliable, people-first content, and that it judges content on its quality, not on how it was produced. Volume without review is what gets sites into trouble, so the review step is the core of the system, not an extra.

Could this work for your business?

If you publish regularly and the work is scattered across tools, probably yes. The setup does not have to be this big. Many teams start with one piece, such as daily topic research or a review queue, and add the rest later. Frequently asked questions

Can SEO be fully automated?

The repetitive parts can: data collection, research, drafting, checks and post-publish tasks. Deciding what matters to your clients and approving sensitive content still needs people, especially in finance, health and legal topics.

Does Google penalise AI-written content?

Google says it rewards helpful content however it is made. The risk comes from publishing large volumes of unreviewed pages. A human review step and fact checks against primary sources address that.

What is the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO?

SEO gets your pages ranked in Google and Bing. AEO gets your content picked as the direct answer in snippets, People also ask boxes and voice assistants. GEO gets your business named and cited when people ask AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity.

How long does SEO automation take to show results?

New pages usually take days to weeks to be indexed, and competitive keywords can take months. Automation speeds up the work; it does not shortcut how search engines rank pages.
