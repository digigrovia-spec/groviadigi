From WhatsApp chats to a custom solar CRM: what changed in 3 months

Leads came in on WhatsApp, follow-ups lived in people’s heads and nobody could see a project once the deal closed. Here is the CRM we built for a rooftop solar installer, the numbers after three months, and the honest lessons.

AT
Abhuday TripathiFounder, Groviadigi · 26 November 2026
8 min read
Short answer

We moved Smart Sun Power, a rooftop solar installer, off WhatsApp chats, phone calls and spreadsheets and onto one custom CRM that covers leads, follow-ups, site surveys, quotations, installation and payments. Within three months lead conversion rose 28%, the sales cycle fell from 45 to 28 days, on-time collections improved by 35% and admin overhead dropped by 30%. The gains came from three plain things: every lead has an owner, every follow-up has a reminder, and every project is visible after the sale.

What changed in three months

The short version: the business stopped losing track of things. Before the CRM, Smart Sun Power’s whole pipeline sat in WhatsApp chats, phone calls, spreadsheets and folders. Three months after go-live, the numbers looked like this.

None of these came from clever technology. They came from making the basics impossible to forget. A lead that arrives on WhatsApp now gets an owner. A promised call-back now has a date and an alert. A signed customer now has an installation record that the owner can open without phoning anyone.

What the business looked like before

Smart Sun Power installs rooftop solar for homes and commercial buildings. A solar sale is not one conversation. It is an enquiry, a call, a site survey, a quotation, often a revised quotation, then procurement, installation, commissioning and payments in stages. Each step involves a different person, and each step can stall.

When we sat down with the sales team, the operations manager and the owner, the same five problems came up again and again.

  • Leads in three places. Enquiries came through WhatsApp, phone calls and the website, and there was no single list of them.
  • Follow-ups from memory. No task list, no reminders. If a salesperson forgot, the lead went cold.
  • Documents everywhere. Survey notes, quotations and customer files were split across chats, email and folders.
  • Nothing after the sale. Once a deal closed, nobody could easily see where procurement, installation or commissioning stood.
  • An owner asking around. Pipeline value, conversion and money still owed were only known by asking people.

This is common. WhatsApp is where Indian customers want to talk, so it is where sales start. The trouble is that a chat app is built for conversations, not for a pipeline. You cannot sort it by stage, set a due date on it or see who owns which customer.

What we built

We did not start from a CRM template. We mapped how a solar job actually moves from first message to final payment, and built the screens around that path.

One place for every lead

Leads from the website, calls and WhatsApp referrals land on one dashboard. Each one gets a sales owner and a recorded source, so you can see which channel brings buyers and which brings time-wasters.

Follow-ups that remind you

After each call the salesperson logs what happened and sets the next action. When that action is due, the system alerts them. This sounds small. In practice it was the feature the sales team noticed first, because it took the job of remembering off them.

Surveys and quotations

Site surveys move from scheduled to completed inside the CRM. Quotation templates fill in the lead and site details automatically and move from draft to final, so nobody retypes a customer’s address or roof size into a new document.

Installation tracking

When a deal closes, it moves straight into installation, with milestones from material procurement to commissioning. Sales can answer a customer’s “when will it be done?” without calling the site team.

Payments in stages

Solar jobs are usually paid in parts. The CRM tracks advance, on-installation and on-completion payments, each with a due date, an alert and the receipt linked to it. That is where the 35% improvement in on-time collections came from: overdue amounts are now on a screen instead of in someone’s head.

A dashboard for the owner

Pipeline value, conversion, sales-cycle length, team performance, profitability and payment status, in real time. The owner no longer has to ask around to know how the month is going.

Why custom, and not an off-the-shelf CRM?

Off-the-shelf CRMs are good products, and for many businesses they are the right answer. For a solar installer, the gap is everything after the sale. Most general CRMs are built around a deal closing. For Smart Sun Power, closing the deal is roughly the halfway point. Surveys, installation milestones and staged payments are the parts that needed to fit their process, and that is where a generic tool would have needed the most workarounds.

There is also the question of ownership. As with all our projects, the client owns the code and the data. There is no per-user licence that grows as the sales team grows.

How we got the team to actually use it

A CRM that nobody opens is just a more expensive spreadsheet. We spent as much care on the switch as on the software.

  • Data migration first. Existing leads and customer records were moved into the CRM before anyone switched over, so nothing was lost and nobody had to keep the old spreadsheet open “just in case”.
  • Hands-on training. Onboarding sessions, written documentation and quick-reference guides for daily tasks.
  • Support after launch. Fixes, updates and new features based on how the team really used it, not how we guessed they would.

The management team summed it up in the case study: “What used to take three people to coordinate manually is now automated.”

What we learned

Start from the process, not the features. The most useful screens were the ones that mirrored a step the team already did, such as a survey or a staged payment. We built the steps they had, not the ones a template assumed.

Reminders beat reports. Dashboards are nice for the owner. Day to day, the thing that moved conversion was a salesperson getting an alert at the right moment. If you only build one feature, build that.

Move the data before you move the people. Teams fall back to the old way when the new system feels empty. Migrating the existing records first made the CRM the obvious place to look on day one.

Keep people on the decisions. The CRM reminds, records and totals. It does not decide which customer to chase or what discount to give. That stays with the team, which is also why they trust it.

Do you need a custom CRM for your small business?

Not always, and we say so on the first call. If you get a handful of enquiries a week and one person handles them, a well-kept spreadsheet with a follow-up date column may be enough for now. If your process is close to a standard sales funnel, an off-the-shelf CRM will probably serve you well.

A custom CRM starts to make sense when:

  • leads come from several channels and some are clearly being missed;
  • your work has steps after the sale, such as surveys, installation, delivery or staged payments;
  • you are paying for workarounds or extra seats to bend a general tool to your process;
  • you want to own the software and the customer data outright.

If WhatsApp is part of your sales, it can stay that way. The official WhatsApp Business Platform lets a CRM receive and send messages through an approved API, so chats can be logged against a customer rather than lost in a phone. And because a CRM holds personal data, it is worth designing it with India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act in mind from the start: collect what you need, control who can see it and keep it where you can account for it. For solar installers specifically, residential buyers often ask about the PM Surya Ghar rooftop scheme, which is one more reason to keep each customer’s documents and stage in one place.

Frequently asked questions

Is a custom CRM worth it for a small business?

It is worth it when your process does not fit a standard sales funnel, when leads arrive through several channels and some are missed, or when you need to track work after the sale such as surveys, installation or staged payments. If you only handle a few enquiries a week, a spreadsheet or an off-the-shelf CRM may be enough, and we will tell you so.

Can a CRM work with WhatsApp?

Yes. Through the official WhatsApp Business Platform API, a CRM can log incoming messages against the right customer and send approved messages from the CRM. In the solar CRM, WhatsApp referrals land on the same lead dashboard as website and phone enquiries.

How quickly does a CRM show results?

For Smart Sun Power, lead conversion rose 28% within three months of go-live and the sales cycle fell from 45 to 28 days. Results depend on how well the CRM fits the process and whether the team uses it daily, which is why data migration and training matter as much as the software.

Will we lose our existing leads when we switch?

No. Existing leads and customer records are migrated into the CRM before the team switches over, so nothing is lost and the new system is useful from the first day.
