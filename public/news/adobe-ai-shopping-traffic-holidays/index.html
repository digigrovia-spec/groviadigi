AI assistants are sending far more holiday shoppers to online stores, Adobe data shows

Adobe's 2026 holiday shopping report shows traffic from AI assistants to US retail sites rising sharply over Thanksgiving and Black Friday. The figures, the caveats and what it means for growing businesses.

G
Groviadigi28 November 2026
3 min read
In short

Adobe's 2026 holiday shopping report, updated over Thanksgiving week, says US online spending reached $12.9 billion on Black Friday, up 9.2% on last year, with Cyber Monday expected to pass $15 billion. Adobe expected traffic to retail sites from AI assistants to rise 159% year on year on Thanksgiving, and early Thanksgiving figures reported on 27 November put the actual rise at 162%. Most shoppers still pay on the retailer's own site; the AI tools mainly do the research.

For years, holiday shoppers started with a search engine or a marketplace. This season a growing share are starting with an AI assistant, asking it to compare products or find a deal, and then clicking through to the shop. Adobe's holiday figures, released over Thanksgiving week, show how quickly that habit is spreading.

Adobe Digital Insights tracks visits to US retail websites. Its 2026 holiday shopping report puts Black Friday online spending at $12.9 billion, up 9.2% on 2025, and expects Cyber Monday on 30 November to reach $15.1 billion, which would be the first single day above $15 billion. Adobe says traffic to retail sites from AI tools grew 127% year on year in August and was forecast to rise 130% across November and December and 159% on Thanksgiving itself. Thanksgiving was also forecast as the biggest mobile shopping day, with 63.2% of spending on phones.

The first Thanksgiving results came out on 27 November. TheFly, via TipRanks, citing Adobe's global retail lead, reported record Thanksgiving online spending of $7.1 billion, AI-referred traffic up 162% on last year, and AI visitors converting to a sale 74% more often than other visitors. Those day-one numbers had not yet appeared on Adobe's own report page when we checked, so treat them as early figures. Adobe's report adds two more points worth noting: 69% of shoppers said they were less likely to return something they bought with an AI assistant's help, and natural search lost revenue share this year while affiliates and social media gained.

What it means for growing businesses

Keep the scale in proportion. A 162% rise is from a small base, and AI assistants still send far fewer visitors than search engines or social media. Adobe counts a visit when someone clicks from an AI tool to a shop, not a purchase made by an AI agent on its own. But the direction is clear, and it is not limited to retail. People now ask an assistant to shortlist accountants, software or suppliers in the same way they ask it to find a gift.

The practical step is to make sure an AI tool can understand what you sell. That means clear service and product pages written in plain language, answers to common questions on the page, accurate prices or price ranges where you publish them, and structured data that machines can read. It also means checking that visits from AI tools are tagged in your analytics, so you can see whether they turn into enquiries. None of this needs a new platform; most of it is careful work on the website you already have.
