AT&T runs nearly 500 AI agent workflows, and every one starts with a human checking it

AT&T says it now has close to 500 agentic AI workflows in production and plans to triple that next year. The more useful lesson for smaller firms is how it keeps people in charge until the agents prove accurate.

G
Groviadigi16 November 2026
3 min read
In short

AT&T has close to 500 agentic AI workflows running in production, handling more than a million transactions a month, according to Luke Corbin of AT&T Business, as reported by SiliconANGLE on 14 November 2026. The company plans to triple that number next year. Every workflow starts under human oversight and only runs on its own once accuracy reaches the mid-to-high 90% range.

Most stories about AI agents are about what they might do. AT&T has shared what it is actually running. Luke Corbin, an assistant vice president for digital transformation and AI at AT&T Business, says the company has nearly 500 agentic workflows in production. The useful part for other companies is the method: people check every workflow before it runs on its own.

According to SiliconANGLE’s case study, published on 14 November, those workflows handle more than a million transactions a month and the company uses about 45 billion AI tokens a day. AT&T wants to triple the number of production workflows next year.

The work runs through a team called the Business Agent Foundry, set up in early 2025. Instead of collecting one-off automation ideas, it maps a whole business process and looks for steps, handoffs and old system dependencies that can be removed. One example given is cutting provisioning time roughly in half for dedicated internet connections where fibre is already in place.

The rules are strict. Every production workflow begins with a person overseeing it, and an agent is only allowed to act on its own once its accuracy reaches the mid-to-high 90% range within an agreed risk level. Monitoring continues afterwards to catch drift. Staff can build personal productivity agents freely, but anything that touches customers must pass security, privacy and compliance reviews first. AI Weekly’s summary reports the same figures. They are AT&T’s own numbers, not independently audited.

What it means for growing businesses

You won’t have 500 workflows or a dedicated AI team, and you don’t need them. What carries over is the method: pick a full process rather than a single task, remove the steps that shouldn’t exist, and keep a person approving the output until the numbers show it can be trusted. That order matters more than which AI model you use.

For a small team, the first workflow is usually something dull and frequent, such as chasing leads that have gone quiet or sorting incoming data into the right place. That is why we usually start a project with one process like this, and why the outbound automation we built for a CFO firm has clear points where a person signs off.
