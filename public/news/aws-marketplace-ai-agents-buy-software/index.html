AWS Marketplace now lets AI agents buy software, within budgets and rules a company sets

On 19 November 2026, AWS added agents to its Marketplace that can research, check and buy software, plus pay-per-request payments for AI agents. Here is what changed and what it means for how businesses buy tools.

G
Groviadigi20 November 2026
3 min read
In short

On 19 November 2026, Amazon Web Services added a set of AI agents to AWS Marketplace that can research, check and buy software for business customers, within a budget and rules the company sets. At the same time, AWS said AI agents can now buy select Marketplace products and pay per request in US dollars, with charges landing on the customer's normal AWS bill. Companies can set spending limits and approved sellers, and choose which purchases still need a person to approve them.

Buying business software is slow. Someone finds a tool, compares prices, reads the licence, checks the vendor's security and waits for approval. On 19 November, Amazon Web Services said AI agents can now do much of that work inside AWS Marketplace, its online store for business software, and in some cases finish the purchase without waiting for a person.

The official part is in AWS's announcement. AI agents can now find and buy select Marketplace products and pay per request in US dollars through AgentCore payments, which is part of Amazon Bedrock AgentCore. Charges go on the customer's existing AWS bill, and no crypto wallet is needed. Customers can set spending limits, approved sellers and other guardrails before an agent spends anything, and each transaction shows up in AWS CloudTrail and Cost Explorer.

Fortune reported the wider rollout: a suite of buying agents, including a checkout assistant that covers pricing, licence terms and whether a purchase order is needed. A company can give agents a budget and decide which purchases go to a person. AWS is also working with Drata and Vanta so buyers can review a vendor's security records inside Marketplace. Fortune cites a G2 survey from June 2026 in which 39% of business software buyers named security reviews as the biggest delay after choosing a vendor. Constellation Research lists the other new tools, including a Trust Agent, a Deployment Agent that the buyer must opt in to, and links with Coupa and SAP Ariba. AWS's Matt Yanchyshyn summed it up plainly: "You're letting agents buy."

What it means for growing businesses

Most growing businesses do not buy software through AWS Marketplace, so the direct effect is small for now. The direction matters more. The large platforms are moving from agents that suggest things to agents that spend money, and the controls they are shipping are the same ones any business should want: a budget, a list of approved suppliers, a record of every transaction and a clear line where a person must say yes. Even the vendors quoted by Fortune expect large deals to keep a human in the loop.
