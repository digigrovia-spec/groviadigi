OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so AI agents can pay, and tells businesses to stop blocking them

OpenAI's new ChatGPT Wallet lets AI agents complete purchases with a card token and a biometric check. Alongside it, OpenAI has published guidance on what online businesses need to change to accept those orders.

G
Groviadigi16 November 2026
3 min read
In short

On 13 November 2026 OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its agents pay for purchases using a payment token instead of the user's real card details, with a Face ID or fingerprint check before every order. It was built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa and is rolling out first to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US. OpenAI also published a Commerce Partner Playbook telling businesses how to make sure their websites and fraud checks don't block these orders.

AI agents have been good at finding and comparing products for a while. Paying has been the awkward part. On 13 November OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, a way for its agents to finish a purchase without ever seeing the customer's card number. For businesses that sell online, the more practical part is the guidance that came with it.

According to OpenAI's announcement, a user adds a card once. Before each purchase they see the items, the total and the chosen card, then approve it with Face ID or a fingerprint. The card network then issues a payment token that stands in for the real card, so neither the agent nor the shop receives the actual card details. Card rewards and protection for eligible disputes still apply, and the shop keeps control of pricing, delivery, returns and customer service.

The wallet was built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. For now it only works through OpenAI's dots agents, for ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 or over in the US with an eligible US-issued card. 9to5Mac's report notes that it will not work on websites that block agents. OpenAI says it later expects users to set spending limits and let agents buy within them without approving each order.

That limit matters because not every company wants agents at the till. United Airlines said on 11 November that it would stop unauthorised third-party AI agents from booking its flights, citing the risk of customers handing over their logins, as TechCrunch reported. OpenAI's answer is a Commerce Partner Playbook that asks businesses to claim their profile in ChatGPT, keep product feeds current, let signed agent requests through their firewall, and talk to their payment and fraud providers so genuine agent orders are not declined. It names Stripe, Adyen and Checkout.com among the payment firms it is working with.

What it means for growing businesses

Very few of your customers will pay through an agent this month. The wallet is US-only and limited to a paid plan. But the direction is clear, and the checklist OpenAI has published is mostly good practice anyway: accurate product data, a site that machines can read, and fraud rules that don't reject an order just because it arrived in an unusual way. If your security setup blocks every automated visitor, it will block these customers too. Whether you want agents buying from you at all is a fair business decision, as United's choice shows. Just make it on purpose rather than by accident.
