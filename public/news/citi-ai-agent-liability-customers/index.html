Citi tells customers they carry the risk if a third-party AI agent misuses their account

In November 2026, Citi added a section on third-party AI agents to its online banking terms: what an agent does with your login counts as authorised by you. What changed and what it means for small businesses.

G
Groviadigi21 November 2026
3 min read
In short

In November 2026, Citi added a new Section H to its Online User Agreement covering third-party AI agents. If a customer gives an outside AI tool their login or account access, Citi will treat what the agent does as authorised by the customer, including after a credential leak, and Citi's own in-app AI tools are excluded. TechCrunch reported on 18 November that PNC and Citizens issued similar warnings that week, after JPMorgan and Bank of America in October.

AI assistants that can read a bank account, pay a bill or move money are arriving fast. The banks are now setting out who pays when one gets it wrong. This month Citi rewrote its online banking terms so that, if you let an outside AI agent into your account, the bank treats its actions as yours.

The change sits in Citi's Online User Agreement, which says it was updated in November 2026 to add a section on third-party AI agents. It cautions customers against using them at all. If you share your online banking login with an agent or give it access, Citi will consider its actions authorised by you, even where they conflict with your instructions, and the same applies if your login leaks to other people or agents. Mistakes, misread instructions and actions beyond what you allowed are your responsibility, unless the law says otherwise. Monitoring the agent is your job, not the bank's, Citi's privacy notice does not cover the agent, and Citi can cut off any third-party agent's access without notice. AI features Citi provides itself are outside the section. The agreement's definition of customers includes sole proprietors and eligible small business users, not only individuals.

Citi is not alone. TechCrunch reported on 18 November that PNC changed its terms the same week and Citizens emailed customers with a similar warning, following JPMorgan and Bank of America in October. A Citi spokesperson told TechCrunch the bank wants customers to understand the risks of sharing banking credentials. PYMNTS also covered the update on 20 November. Whether US consumer payment rules can override these terms has not yet been tested.

What it means for growing businesses

If you run a small business, the practical lesson is simple: do not hand your bank login to an AI tool, however useful it looks. Under terms like these, a leak or a wrong payment is your loss, and the bank has said so in writing. Read-only access through a bank's own approved connections is a very different thing from an agent holding your password.

The wider point applies to any automation that touches money or customer data. Decide in advance what software may do on its own, keep a record of every action, and put a person in front of anything that cannot be undone.
