Gartner: only 21% of service leaders have cut agent headcount because of AI

A Gartner survey of 275 service and support leaders finds AI is mostly supporting customer service teams, not replacing them. Here are the numbers and what they mean for your business.

G
Groviadigi14 November 2026
3 min read
In short

Gartner’s November 2026 survey of 275 customer service and support leaders found that only 21% have reduced human agent headcount because of AI. Separately, Gartner analysts reported that just 4% of service leaders have seen the return on AI they expected, which points to AI working best alongside people rather than replacing them.

For all the talk of AI replacing support teams, most companies have not cut their customer service staff. That is the finding of a Gartner survey published on 5 November, which asked 275 service and support leaders, in July and August 2026, whether AI had led them to reduce human agents.

Only 21% said yes. That is barely up from 20% in Gartner’s survey a year earlier. Among the companies that did cut, the average reduction was about 16.7% of the service workforce over 18 months, according to CMSWire’s coverage.

The returns tell a similar story. At a Gartner conference, analysts said just 4% of service leaders have seen the return on AI they expected, and only about a quarter report any positive return so far. Around 7% have already rehired staff they let go, and Gartner warned against a “cut fast, rehire later” approach based on expected rather than proven savings.

What it means for growing businesses

The lesson is not that AI fails. It is that AI pays off when it removes specific, repetitive work and leaves judgement with people. Cutting staff first and hoping the software catches up is where the returns disappear.

For a small team, the practical version looks like this: automate the reminder, the data entry or the weekly report, and let your people spend the saved time on customers.
