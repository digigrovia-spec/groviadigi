IBM and Microsoft launch Imagine Enterprise, an AI agent platform with 6,000+ prebuilt agents

IBM and Microsoft have announced Imagine Enterprise, a platform for building, running and governing AI agents across business processes. Here is what it includes and what it means for smaller companies.

G
Groviadigi15 November 2026
3 min read
In short

On 10 November 2026, IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise, a joint platform for building, running and governing AI agents tied to industry processes. It combines IBM Consulting’s library of more than 6,000 prebuilt agents and assets with Microsoft’s cloud, data, identity and security layers. Pricing and customer results have not been published yet.

Two of the largest enterprise technology companies are betting that businesses want AI agents that come ready-made for their industry, rather than built from a blank page. IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise on 10 November, a platform that lets companies pick, run and govern AI agents for specific business processes.

The core of the offer is IBM Consulting’s inventory of more than 6,000 prebuilt AI agents and assets. Microsoft supplies the layers underneath: AI models, data, cloud hosting, identity and security. For regulated industries, the companies say the platform includes controls, observability and support for AI compliance.

Clients can choose how hands-on to be. IBM Consulting can deploy and operate the platform for them, work alongside them on large rollouts, or let them run it themselves. The companies plan to extend it into manufacturing, energy, the public sector and mobility, but have not given dates.

What is missing so far is just as telling. Coverage of the launch notes that pricing, adoption numbers and measured results from production use have not been disclosed. Some analysts also read it as a challenge to other agent-building tools, including Microsoft’s own Copilot Studio, though that is commentary rather than a stated plan.

What it means for growing businesses

Platforms like this are aimed at large enterprises with consulting budgets. The useful signal for a smaller company is the direction of travel: agents are becoming packaged around specific processes (claims, onboarding, procurement) with governance built in, instead of being general chatbots.

You don’t need a 6,000-agent library to get the same benefit. Most small and mid-sized teams get more from one well-built automation on a process they run every day, such as lead follow-ups or report building, with a person approving the steps that matter.
