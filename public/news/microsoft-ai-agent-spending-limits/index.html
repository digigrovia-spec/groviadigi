Microsoft adds spending limits for AI agents as company AI bills grow harder to track

At Ignite on 17 November 2026, Microsoft announced Agent 365 Cost Management, which lets admins set spending limits and alerts for usage-based AI services and agents. Here is what it does and why AI costs deserve a plan.

G
Groviadigi19 November 2026
3 min read
In short

On 17 November 2026, at its Ignite conference, Microsoft announced Agent 365 Cost Management, a place in the Microsoft admin centre to see and control spending on usage-based AI services such as Copilot Studio agents and its new Autopilot agent. Admins can set spending limits and alerts per user, group, organisation or resource; these controls are in preview, with forecasting in the Frontier programme this month and more features due in December. Microsoft also launched Agent 365 Business Analytics to link AI spend to the work it actually gets done.

AI agents are usually sold per seat, but more and more of what they do is billed by use. Every task an agent runs costs something, and those costs land in different teams' budgets. At its Ignite conference on 17 November, Microsoft put spending controls at the centre of its agent announcements.

According to Microsoft's Ignite Book of News, Agent 365 Cost Management sits in the Microsoft admin centre and shows what is driving spend across usage-based services. Admins can set spending policies, limits and alerts, which are in preview. Forecasting, plain-language questions and cost-saving recommendations are coming to the Frontier early-access programme this month. Insights across Microsoft and non-Microsoft agents, and the option to hand budgets to business leaders, are due in December. The Agent 365 team's blog adds that admins can also choose which AI models each team may use, keeping the expensive ones for work that needs them.

The second piece is Agent 365 Business Analytics, now in the Frontier programme. It puts AI spend next to measures such as hours of work assisted and success rate. Insights for Microsoft services need at least one Microsoft 365 Copilot licence in the organisation. In its Copilot announcement, Microsoft cites BCG research that corporate AI spending doubled in a year, with over 80% of it now outside the IT budget. UC Today's review of the first day said Microsoft has built the meter but has not yet shown the receipts, since the analytics and the Autopilot agent are both still in early access.

What it means for growing businesses

Most of this is aimed at large companies on Microsoft 365, and much of it is still in preview. The lesson travels further, though. When an AI tool charges per task, the bill grows with use, and nobody notices until the invoice arrives. If you are adding AI features, agents or automation platforms to your business, ask three questions first: how is this billed, who can see the spend, and what stops it running away?
